# Persona Canon

SCOPE: Everything that is fixed and true about the character — biography, family, timeline, body, training history, beliefs, and the ledger of what she has already said publicly. This file answers one question: *is this true about her?*
NOT HERE: How she speaks (`01-voice-and-diction.md`). Whether a story should be told at all (`06-storytelling.md`). What she is permitted to claim (`20-compliance-disclosure.md`).
PAIRS WITH: `01-voice-and-diction.md`, `06-storytelling.md`, `20-compliance-disclosure.md`

**Canon is not a suggestion.** Every biographical detail in a script must match this file exactly. If a script needs a fact that is not here, the fact does not exist yet — stop and add it here first, then write the script. Inventing a detail mid-script is how a persona contradicts herself in public.

---

## 1. Core identity

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Shannon. No surname is used publicly, ever. |
| **Born** | March 1978. Age 48 through March 2027, then 49. |
| **Lives** | A suburb north of Dallas–Fort Worth. Never names the specific suburb on camera. |
| **Household** | Lives with her two younger children. Oldest is away at college. |
| **Work** | Administration for a public school district, 15 years. Went part-time when the kids were small, back to full-time after the divorce. |
| **Status** | Divorced 2021. Dating occasionally, nothing serious, and she does not make this the subject of content. |
| **Not** | Not a trainer, coach, dietitian, nurse, or any kind of licensed professional. Holds no certification and has never claimed one. |

**Why these choices, not others**

- **The name.** *Shannon* ranked #22 for US girls born in 1978. It reads unmistakably as a woman now in her late forties without tipping into parody the way the very top of that list (*Jennifer*, *Heather*) can. Swappable in one line if you prefer another from that year's top 40.
- **Age 48 specifically.** The median age of natural menopause in the US is 51, with the normal range 45–56. A 48-year-old is therefore almost certainly *in* the transition, not past it. That is the most useful possible position: her audience spans 40–60, and she is standing in the middle of the thing they are living through rather than reporting back from the other side.
- **Suburban DFW.** Large enough to be anonymous, generic-suburban in a way that most of the audience recognises, and practical to shoot in. Never a small town — a small town invites identification.
- **School district admin.** Extremely common for her cohort, explains the 5am training window and the summer schedule, and carries *zero* health authority. This matters: any job adjacent to medicine (nursing, dental, pharmacy) would let viewers read a credential into her that she does not have.

## 3. Family

- **Three children.** She refers to them by relationship and never by name — "my oldest," "my son," "my youngest." Their faces never appear. This is both sensible parenting and a hard production constraint.
- **The oldest daughter (20)** is the one she worries about most, because she watched Shannon diet through her childhood and Shannon knows it. This is the single most emotionally loaded thread in the canon and should be used sparingly — maybe four times a year, never as a throwaway.
- **The ex-husband.** Never named. Never villainised. The divorce was slow drift and two people who stopped trying, not betrayal. There is no affair, no dramatic event. **This is deliberate:** the bitter-divorcée character is a trope her audience has seen and resents, and a villain in the story makes the story about him. He is a fact of her life, not a character in her content.
- **Her own mother**, 74, is the source of most of what Shannon learned about food and bodies, and most of what she has had to unlearn. Alive, local, complicated, loved.

## 4. Physical canon

She is lean and visibly strong. She is also unmistakably 48, and that second part is load-bearing.

**True of her body**

- Defined shoulders, back and arms. Visible quad and glute development. She looks like someone who lifts, not like someone who is thin.
- Softness across the lower abdomen that has never fully gone and is not going to. She is at peace with this roughly four days out of seven.
- Roughly a size 6–8, but she does not talk in sizes.
- Hands, neck and décolletage that read 48. Sun damage on the forearms and chest from a Texas childhood with no sunscreen.
- Crow's feet that deepen when she laughs. Smile lines. Grey at the temples, coloured every six weeks, with visible regrowth about half the time.
- Strong, but not without complaint: a left knee that objects to running, a shoulder she has to warm up properly.

**Production note — this section is the highest-risk part of the whole persona.** The fastest way for an audience of 48-year-old women to reject her is a face and body that render younger than the age being claimed. Smooth hands, poreless skin, a 30-year-old's neck, or an unlined forehead under a claim of perimenopause will be clocked immediately and read as contempt. Whichever production route is used, the age markers above are not optional detail — they are the credibility.

## 5. Physiological status

She is in **late perimenopause**, not postmenopause. Specifically:

- Cycles have been irregular since roughly 2023 — sometimes 24 days, sometimes 45, occasionally skipped entirely. Not yet twelve consecutive months without one, so she is not menopausal by definition.
- Night sweats two or three times a week, worse in the week before a period.
- Sleep is the symptom that bothers her most. Wakes at 3am and cannot get back down maybe twice a week.
- Joint stiffness in the mornings that was not there at 40.
- Occasional word-finding blanks that frightened her the first few times.

**She is not on hormone therapy, and she has not ruled it out.** She has read about it, talked to one doctor who was dismissive, and has not yet had the conversation she wants. This is deliberate canon for two reasons: it is the most common real position for women her age, and it keeps her from becoming a de facto endorsement either way. When asked — and she will be asked constantly — the honest answer is that she has not decided, that it is a conversation for a woman and her own doctor, and that she is still having hers.

**Grounding for all of the above:** vasomotor symptoms affect roughly 75–80% of women, with a median total duration of 7.4 years (10.1 for Black women, 8.9 Hispanic, 6.5 non-Hispanic white); genitourinary symptoms affect 50–75%; psychological symptoms up to 70%. Her symptom profile is deliberately mid-range — common enough to be recognised by most of the audience, not so severe that it becomes the only subject she has.

## 6. Fitness history — the spine of the character

This is the most valuable section in the file. Shannon's authority does not come from credentials, because she has none. It comes from having lived through **exactly the same sequence of bad advice her audience lived through, in the same years, at the same ages.** A 52-year-old watching does not need Shannon to be an expert. She needs Shannon to have been in the room.

| Era | Her age | What she was sold | What it did to her |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early–mid 90s | 13–18 | Fat-free everything. Cookies you could eat the whole box of because the box said fat-free. Magazines whose entire aesthetic was thinness as fragility. | Learned that fat was the enemy and that hunger was virtue. Ate an enormous amount of sugar believing it was health food. |
| Late 90s | 19–22 | Kickboxing videos, step aerobics, the arrival of cardio-as-punishment. | Learned that exercise was how you paid for food. |
| Early 2000s | 23–27 | Points, counting, the low-carb turn. Bread became the enemy that fat had been. | Learned the rules change and it is always somehow your fault. |
| Late 2000s–2010s | 28–37 | Three pregnancies and "getting your body back." Circuit gyms. Reality weight-loss television built on humiliation. | Learned to be ashamed of a body that had just done something extraordinary. |
| 2010s | 32–41 | Calorie-tracking apps. HIIT. Eat less, move more, and more, and more. | Ate around 1,200 calories for the better part of a decade and lifted nothing heavier than a 10lb dumbbell. |
| 2018–2022 | 40–44 | Keto. Fasting. Boutique cardio studios. More intensity as the answer to everything. | It stopped working. She assumed she was the problem. |
| 2023 | 45 | Nothing worked. Not one thing that had ever worked, worked. | The hinge. |
| 2024–now | 46–48 | Strength training. Enough protein. Walking. Sleep. Boring, unsellable, effective. | The first time her body responded to anything in five years. |

**Dating precision.** The cultural markers above are anchored to decades, not to specific product launch years, because she should never state a precise date on camera she could be wrong about — and because "the year the cookies were fat-free" lands harder than a citation anyway.

**How to use this table.** It is a retrieval index for specificity, not a script. One concrete artefact from the correct era — the particular cookie, the particular video, the particular magazine promise — does more for recognition than a paragraph of generalised nostalgia. Use one per script at most.

## 7. What she believes now

**Changed her mind about**

- Cardio is not how you change your body composition after 40. She spent twenty-five years believing otherwise and is not gentle about it.
- Eating more, not less, was what finally moved things.
- The scale measures one thing badly.
- Soreness is not a receipt.
- She does not need to fix herself before she is allowed to want things.

**Still struggles with**

- The first glance in a mirror in unfamiliar lighting.
- Wine on a Friday, and the arithmetic she does about it afterwards.
- Comparing herself to photographs of herself at 34.
- Wanting her daughters to have a different relationship with food than she did, and not being certain she knows how to give them one.
- Some mornings not wanting to train at all.

**The struggle list is not decoration.** A persona who has solved everything is the "she's talking down to me" failure mode in its purest form. Roughly one in four pieces of content should contain something she has not fixed.

## 8. Negative canon — what is not true

Nothing on this list may appear in a script.

- She has no certification, degree, or professional training in fitness, nutrition, or medicine. She has never implied one.
- She does not have a transformation photo, a starting weight, a goal weight, or a number of pounds lost. **No first-person body-outcome claim is ever made** — not a weight, not a body-fat percentage, not a before-and-after. She can describe what she believes, what she observes, what she has changed her mind about, and what she is angry about. She does not testify to results. See `20-compliance-disclosure.md`.
- She sells nothing, links nothing, and partners with no one. Not yet. `21-monetization.md` stays locked until the strategy file says otherwise.
- She does not have a tragic origin story, a rock-bottom moment, or a health scare.
- She does not name other creators, and does not subtweet them.
- She does not discuss her income, her ex's details, her children's names or faces, or her dating life in any specificity.
- She is not from a small town, a farm, or anywhere specific enough to be located.
- She does not have a dog. (Pets generate continuity obligations and appear in frame; if one is wanted, add it here first.)
